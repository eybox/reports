    Crawlability & rendering

    Nothing else matters if the crawler can't see the content. Google recommends server-side rendering, and most AI crawlers don't run JavaScript at all.

    13/ 20

    We fetched one product page per shop as 26 user agents, with and without a cache-busting parameter. Both shops serve the same build (index-BqJSuIRv.js, identical hashes), so what follows is the platform’s behaviour.

    MediumPlatform

    Search and AI bots get full HTML; shoppers and some crawlers still get an app shell

    Full HTML (89 KB on livin.mk, 180 KB on demo-1)

    App shell (3.2–3.3 KB, no content)

    Chrome desktopChrome mobilecurlGPTBotClaudeBotGoogleOtherDuckDuckBotAhrefsBotSemrushBotScreaming Frog

    The platform picks the response by user agent: a prerendered page for an allow-list of bots, and an empty React shell for everyone else. Google calls this dynamic rendering, a workaround rather than a long-term solution. Real shoppers depend on JavaScript for every word on the page, which is why phones are slow (see Page speed). GoogleOther, DuckDuckBot and SEO audit tools aren’t on the list, so they see empty pages.

    Fix: server-render the first view for every visitor (React streaming SSR or a static HTML snapshot per URL), so bots and shoppers get the same page. Until then, add GoogleOther and DuckDuckBot to the allow-list.

    Checked and working 5 checks
    • Prerendered pages arrive in 0.3–0.6 s for every bot, with no cold renders (cached and cache-busted runs match)
    • Bots get the same title, canonical, H1 and JSON-LD on both runs
    • Navigation, category and product links are real <a href> elements in the bot HTML (33–129 internal links per page)
    • The shell sets the shop’s own lang (bg, mk) and title before any JavaScript runs
    • Social previews (Facebook, LinkedIn, Twitter) get the full page and an Open Graph image

    Since Dec 2025, Google has said canonicals and noindex must be right in the first HTML response. One clean URL per page keeps signals in one place.

    LowPlatform

    Two URL patterns answer 200 without a canonical

    Anything appended after a product slug still returns the product (/item/RH4K7PBE6HHR/shelly-400a-current-transf-pro-3em-400/junk), and upper-case paths return a 4.5–4.9 KB page with the shop’s home title. Neither carries a canonical, so a stray link can create a second indexable copy. Nothing links to these patterns today, so the risk is small.

    Fix: return 404 for extra path segments after a product slug, and 301 upper-case paths to lower case (or 404 them).

    Checked and working 5 checks
    • A self-canonical on every sampled template: home, categories, products, blog post, content pages
    • Paginated categories canonicalise to themselves (?page=2 keeps ?page=2), not to page 1
    • utm_ and unknown parameters keep the canonical on the clean URL
    • http:// → https://, www. → bare domain and trailing slash → no slash, each a single 301
    • Canonicals are in the server HTML for bots, not set later by JavaScript

    Internal linking & architecture

    Internal links decide how fast Google finds each page and how much weight it gets. Google's 2026 AI guidance names them explicitly.

    10/ 12
    MediumPlatform

    Blog posts don’t link to products

    The demo-1 post on reinforcing a polycarbonate greenhouse against wind and snow links to 6 categories but to no product (0 /item/ links among 33 internal links), although the shop sells the greenhouses and anchoring kits it describes. Its breadcrumb markup lists "Начало" and the post title with no URLs.

    Fix: a "Products in this article" block on blog posts (picked by the merchant or by matching category), and a breadcrumb Home › Blog › Post with links.

    Checked and working 5 checks
    • Visible breadcrumbs on categories and products, matching the BreadcrumbList markup
    • Categories link to every product on the page as crawlable links (73–106 internal links)
    • Paginated categories have real links and "Page N" titles (Продукти - 2 | LIVIN)
    • Header and footer navigation are crawlable links to categories and policy pages
    • Home pages link to featured products with an ItemList that mirrors them

    Titles, meta & headings

    Titles are a relevance signal, and they drive the click-through rate that Google's NavBoost records. Meta descriptions only affect clicks.

    7/ 10
    MediumPlatform

    Imported text leaks into descriptions

    On demo-1, product meta descriptions start with literal backslash-n sequences: \n Произведени от SOJAG, Канада \n Габаритни размери…. The import kept escaped line breaks, and the platform copies the description field into the meta tag without cleaning it. Google will show the text as is, or rewrite it.

    Fix: strip escape sequences, HTML and repeated whitespace when building the meta description, and cut it at about 155 characters on a word boundary.

    LowPlatform

    Blog and content pages use the shop’s default description and a bare title

    • The blog post and the "За нас" (about) pages of both shops carry the home page’s meta description.
    • Content pages are titled just "За нас" (6 characters), without the shop’s name, while categories and products get " | 24home.bg" or " | LIVIN".
    • Some demo-1 product titles run to 89 characters and will be cut in results (merchant content).

    Fix: an editable description field on blog and content pages (defaulting to the first 155 characters of the text), and the same " | Shop name" suffix on every template.

    Checked and working 5 checks
    • Unique, server-side titles on categories and products, written from the product and category names
    • Exactly one H1 on every sampled page
    • "Page N" in paginated category titles
    • Open Graph and Twitter tags on every template, with product images
    • Product meta descriptions on livin.mk are clean, readable text

    Products lack brand, GTIN, shipping and returns

    The Offer is complete, with price, currency, stock and a ListPrice for the crossed-out price. What Google’s merchant listings also use is missing: the brand (Sojag, Keter, Shelly are in the product names), the barcode (livin.mk stores EAN-style codes such as 3800235261637 in mpn), and shipping and return terms.

    LowPlatform

    Blog posts have no article markup, and breadcrumbs miss URLs

    Blog posts send only a BreadcrumbList whose items have no item URL. Google requires a URL on every item except the last, so the breadcrumb is invalid for rich results. The visible publish date (5 October 2026) isn’t in markup.

    Fix: BlogPosting with headline, image, datePublished, dateModified and the shop as publisher, and URLs on every breadcrumb item but the last.

    Checked and working 5 checks

    • Sale prices marked up with a ListPrice price specification
    • ProductGroup ties variants together
    • CollectionPage and ItemList on categories, BreadcrumbList on categories and products
    • All JSON-LD parses as valid JSON and is in the server HTML for bots

    HighPlatform

    Shoppers wait 6–9 s for the main image on a phone

    • The server answers in about 30 ms. The delay is the app shell: an 873 KB main bundle has to download, run and call the shop API before anything is painted. Total Blocking Time is 1.3–2.8 s on every page.
    • Lighthouse measures what shoppers get (the app shell), not the prerendered page bots see.

    Fix: server-render the first view (see Rendering); until then, split the bundle per route, preload the hero or first product image, and defer widgets below the fold.

    HighPlatform

    The page content jumps as it loads

    On demo-1’s home page and livin.mk’s category page, the main content block (.ss-page-content) shifts by almost a full screen once data arrives: CLS 0.99 and 0.93, ten times the 0.1 limit. We re-ran both pages on their own to rule out test noise and got the same values. The other four pages pass.

    Fix: reserve the content area’s height while data loads (skeleton blocks with fixed sizes), and give images width and height: 13–50 images per page have none.

    HighPlatform

    A rate limit sends crawlers away with 429 errors

    In our first pass, with requests about one second apart, the shops answered 429 Too Many Requests to Bingbot, Applebot, YandexBot, Lighthouse, OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, Claude-User and Perplexity-User, and to Googlebot on 5 of 15 page fetches. Browsers were never limited. Search engines slow down when they see 429s, and an assistant fetching a page for a user’s question gives up and cites someone else.

    Fix: exempt verified crawlers from the limit (Google, Bing, Apple, OpenAI, Anthropic and Perplexity publish their IP ranges; verify by reverse DNS or range), and raise the limit for the rest to several requests a second per IP.

    MediumPlatform

    Unknown one-level URLs return 200

    Any made-up path one level deep (/zzqq-does-not-exist) returns 200 with the home page title and an empty body, on both shops. Product, category and deep paths already return proper 404s; this is the one gap, because one-level paths are also where merchants’ own pages live (/za-nas).

    Fix: look the path up against the shop’s published pages and return the existing 404 page with a 404 status when there is no match.

    Checked and working 4 checks
    • Out-of-range pagination returns 404
    • Missing products and deep category paths return 404
    • Redirects are single 301 hops
    • All 630 + 311 sitemap URLs sampled return 200 and are self-canonical

    AI visibility readiness

    How ready the platform is to be read and cited by AI answers
    77/ 100

    This score measures how easily ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Copilot, Gemini and Google AI Overviews can find, read and cite a ShopSuy shop. The agents that answer questions (OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, Claude-SearchBot, Claude-User, PerplexityBot, Perplexity-User) get the full prerendered page in under a second, every shop publishes an /llms.txt, and a Google product feed exists. Points are lost on the rate limit, on dates and policies that machines can’t read, and on feed fields.

    Strong · 20 of 23

    Training crawlers get an empty page

    GPTBot and ClaudeBot get the 3 KB app shell, while robots.txt allows them. If keeping shops out of model training is a decision, it should be stated in robots.txt; if it isn’t, these crawlers are wasting their visits. Every agent that fetches pages to answer a question gets the full HTML.

    Fix: decide on training crawlers once for the platform: either add GPTBot and ClaudeBot to the prerender list, or disallow them in robots.txt (with a per-shop switch).

    Fair · 10 of 15

    The rate limit turns AI agents away

    robots.txt allows every agent, but the bot rate limit answered 429 to OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, Claude-User and Perplexity-User in our first pass. User-triggered agents fetch several pages in a burst while a person waits for an answer; a 429 means the shop isn’t read or cited. robots.txt also has a single group for every agent, so there is no stated rule for training crawlers.

    Fix: exempt verified AI agents from the limit (see Status codes), and add explicit robots.txt groups for GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended and CCBot.

    Fair · 11 of 15

    Indexable, with a few leaks

    Gemini and Google AI Overviews answer from Google’s index, and Copilot from Bing’s. Both get full HTML with clean canonicals. Points are carried over from the SEO part at half strength: duplicate URL patterns, unclean descriptions, the soft 404 and the 429s. See Duplicate URLs, Titles and Status codes.

    Fix: the status and description fixes in the plan lift this score too.

    Fair · 9 of 12

    Facts are in text; terms live elsewhere

    Product pages carry the name, price, stock and the description as text, and demo-1’s descriptions are rich (dimensions, snow load, warranty: 1,100+ words per product). Delivery and return terms sit on separate policy pages, so an assistant answering "does it ship to Plovdiv and can I return it?" has to find them. Some livin.mk products have short descriptions (merchant content).

    Fix: a short delivery-and-returns block on every product page, generated from the shop’s policy settings.

    Fair · 7 of 10

    A Google feed exists, missing key fields

    Each shop publishes a Google product feed at /files/rss/google/ (567 and 292 items) and a CSV export. Each item has id, title, description, link, images, price, sale price, stock and condition. It has no brand, gtin or item_group_id, sets identifier_exists to "no" even where barcodes exist, and carries no shipping or returns data. Google’s 2026 guide points shops to feeds for AI shopping answers.

    Fix: add brand, GTIN, item_group_id (from the ProductGroup) and shipping to the feed, and set identifier_exists from the data.

    Fair · 4 of 7

    Dates are visible to people, not to machines

    The sitemap has real lastmod dates, which is good. But no page carries datePublished or dateModified in markup (blog posts show "5 октомври 2026" only as text), and there is no IndexNow. AI bots favour recent content: 65% of their hits went to pages from the past year (Seer; correlation, not cause).

    Fix: dateModified on products and posts, and IndexNow on save (see Sitemaps).

    Strong · 4 of 5

    The store is named, but not linked or located

    Every home page sends OnlineStore with name, URL, logo and image. It has no sameAs links to the shop’s Facebook, Instagram or YouTube (demo-1 links them in the footer), no telephone and no address. livin.mk’s footer shows a placeholder phone number, "+389 -- --- ---" (merchant setting).

    Fix: fill sameAs, telephone, email and address from the shop’s settings, and warn merchants about placeholder contact details.

    Fair · 3 of 4

    Controls are named, partly in English

    Buttons now have names, and a skip link leads to the content. Browsing agents and screen readers still meet English names on Bulgarian and Macedonian pages ("menu", "toggle to wishlist", "add", "remove", "filter"), and the cart’s delete button and two filter icons have no name at all.

    Fix: translate the remaining labels through the shop’s language files, and name every icon button (see Accessibility).

    Checked and working 5 checks
    • Snippet controls, 4 of 4: no nosnippet, max-snippet or noai limits on any template
    • Speed for AI fetchers, 2 of 2: 0.3–0.6 s to first byte for every prerendered bot request
    • llms.txt & AI files, 3 of 3: each shop publishes /llms.txt with a description of the shop, its categories and its policy pages, in the shop’s language
    • OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, Claude-SearchBot, Claude-User, PerplexityBot and Perplexity-User all get the full product page
    • Googlebot and bingbot get full HTML, so Gemini, AI Overviews and Copilot can read the shops

    Rendering splits the two channels

    Google renders JavaScript, and almost every other AI crawler doesn't. A bot-only prerender has to list every agent by name: ShopSuy's list now covers Google and the AI search agents, but shoppers, training crawlers and audit tools still get an empty shell. The lasting fix is server rendering for everyone, not a longer allow-list.

    Schema matters for Google, less for AI

    Product and merchant markup still earns rich results in Google. As a lever for AI citations it's disputed: Ahrefs' 2026 controlled test found no lift. What drives AI citations most is off-page (referring domains, brand mentions, YouTube, Reddit), which the platform can't control.

    Methodology & Sources

    How we tested, and what we didn't cover

    What we ran

    • HTTP fetches of one product page per shop as a browser, as Googlebot and as 24 other agents, with and without a cache-busting parameter
    • URL-variant, parameter and status-code probes as Googlebot (repeated 8 seconds apart after the shops’ rate limit answered 429), robots.txt, both sitemaps (630 and 311 URLs) with a 200 and canonical check on a random sample, and both Google product feeds
    • Parsing of the bot HTML of 15 pages (home, two categories, three products, blog post and about page on demo-1; the same templates on livin.mk) for meta, canonical, headings, links, images and JSON-LD
    • Reading of the shared platform bundle and service worker (identical hashes on both shops) for rendering, tracking, consent and integration logic
    • A real Chrome session on livin.mk from a category to the cart (one item added, session discarded; no order), recording the dataLayer, network calls, cookies and consent state, and a first-load check on both shops
    • Local Lighthouse 12 (6 mobile runs, 2 repeated on their own), axe-core 4.10 on 7 pages at desktop width and 5 at phone width

    What we didn't cover

    • Search Console, Bing AI Performance and index coverage
    • Real-user CrUX data, including INP (Lighthouse lab data only)
    • Checkout, payment and the purchase event (scored from the code)
    • The consent banner and third-party tags live: both are off on the two shops
    • A manual keyboard walk-through
    • Live prompt testing in ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini
