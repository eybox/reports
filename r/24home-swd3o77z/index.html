    Canonicals & duplicate URLs

    Since Dec 2025, Google has said canonicals and noindex must be right in the first HTML response. Every duplicate splits ranking signals and wastes crawl budget.

    4/ 9
    High

    Categories and archives have no canonical

    • Products, posts, pages and home: self-canonical. WordPress adds these by itself.
    • Product categories (/продукти/перголи/, /продукти/оранжерии/) and other taxonomy archives: no canonical. WordPress leaves archives to an SEO plugin, and the shop has none.
    • In a 14-URL sitemap sample, the 2 taxonomy pages were the only ones without a self-canonical.

    Fix: install one SEO plugin (Rank Math or Yoast). It adds self-canonicals to categories, tags and brand archives, and handles the title, description, schema and sitemap fixes below.

    High

    Every page also answers on http://

    Only the home page redirects from http:// to https://. The sampled product page answers 200 on both, with no redirect, so every URL exists twice. The canonical points to https and limits the damage, but categories have no canonical.

    Fix: a site-wide 301 from http to https at the server or host level, then HSTS (see Mobile & HTTPS).

    Checked and working 6 checks
    • www.24home.bg 301-redirects to the bare domain in one hop
    • A missing trailing slash 301-redirects to the slash form
    • Unknown parameters on products keep the canonical to the clean URL
    • Upper-case paths return 404, so there are no case duplicates
    • Products, posts and pages carry an absolute self-canonical
    • No stray noindex on indexable pages

    High

    Sort, brand and price URLs are open to crawling

    Every sort order, brand filter and price range is its own indexable URL with a full product list, and none says which page is the original. robots.txt only blocks add-to-cart links. With Filter Everything Pro installed, filters can combine into thousands of near-identical pages per category.

    Fix: canonical to the clean category on sort and price URLs, and noindex, follow on filter combinations that aren't chosen landing pages. Add robots.txt rules only after Google has dropped them, or it will never see the noindex.

    Medium

    The page cache serves an ad-click copy of Pergolas

    The cached /продукти/перголи/ page carries a Google Ads click ID (gclid=EAIaIQob…) and utm tags inside its pagination links, three times on the page. Every visitor and crawler gets this copy, and the same copy answers ?gclid= and ?utm_source=. Googlebot follows those links and finds new parameter URLs.

    Fix: purge the cache, and set the cache plugin to ignore gclid, utm_* and fbclid in its cache key and in generated links.

    Low

    No brand or filter landing pages

    Searches like "Sojag пергола" or "поликарбонатна оранжерия 3х4" have no page of their own. The brand taxonomy (pa_марка) has a sitemap entry, but its file returns 404 (see Sitemaps). Filter Everything Pro can publish chosen filter sets as SEO pages; none are in use.

    Fix: publish a few brand × category pages with their own title, text, canonical and sitemap entry. Keep every other combination out of the index.

    Checked and working 4 checks
    • Variable products are one product with options and one URL, not duplicate products
    • Pages past the end return 404 (?paged=999, /page/99/)
    • ?paged=N 301-redirects to the clean /page/N/ form
    • Add-to-cart URLs are blocked in robots.txt

    Internal linking & architecture

    Internal links decide how fast Google finds each product and how much weight it gets. Google's 2026 AI guidance names them explicitly.

    11/ 12
    Low

    70 products live under an internal "brochure" path

    Products such as the Sojag Verona pergola sit at /магазин/брошура-bg/…, an internal category, while their breadcrumb shows the real one (Перголи). The URL, the canonical and the sitemap carry "брошура-bg", so the URL hierarchy and the visible hierarchy disagree for 70 products.

    Fix: set the real category as the primary category, so it drives the URL, and 301 the old URLs. Plan it as one change, since it moves 70 URLs.

    Checked and working 5 checks
    • Crawlable menus: 151 internal links on the home page, 212 on the Pergolas category
    • Visible breadcrumbs on category and product pages
    • Related-product blocks on product pages
    • Pagination uses real, crawlable /page/N/ links
    • Footer links to about, delivery, returns, payment and contact pages

    Titles, meta & headings

    Titles are a relevance signal, and they drive the click-through rate that Google's NavBoost records. Meta descriptions only affect clicks.

    6/ 10
    Medium

    No meta descriptions or social tags anywhere

    None of the 8 templates has a meta description, Open Graph or Twitter tags. Google writes its own snippet from page text, and a shared product link shows whatever image Facebook happens to pick.

    Fix: description and social templates per page type in the SEO plugin, with hand-written ones for the home page and top categories.

    Medium

    Headings and long product titles

    • The home page has no H1. /продукти/оранжерии/ has two ("Оранжерии" twice).
    • Product titles run 87–98 characters, e.g. "Оранжерия SUNNY 18 кв м 6х3х2м UV+, с 2 врати, 12 прозореца, подсилена 44.5 kg…". Google shows about 60.

    Fix: one H1 per template, and a shorter SEO title (name, size, "– 24home") while the full name stays as the H1.

    Checked and working 5 checks
    • Titles are unique and written in the server HTML
    • Category titles are clear: "Перголи – 24home"
    • Product title matches the product H1
    • Blog titles are questions people search for ("Видове оранжерии- коя да избера?")
    • max-image-preview:large allows large image previews in results

    Structured data

    Product and merchant markup earns rich results in Google, such as price, stock, shipping and returns. That improves click-through, not rank. As a lever for AI citations, the 2026 evidence is weak.

    5/ 10
    Medium

    Good product markup; breadcrumb, shop and merchant data missing

    • Products have no brand, gtin or mpn, and no shippingDetails or hasMerchantReturnPolicy. Since Nov 2025 shipping and returns can be set once, on Organization.
    • No BreadcrumbList, although every product and category shows a breadcrumb.
    • No Organization or WebSite on the home page.
    • Variable products use AggregateOffer with no ProductGroup.
    • Blog posts have no Article, categories no ItemList.

    Fix: the SEO plugin adds Organization, WebSite, BreadcrumbList and Article. Brand (from the "марка" attribute), GTIN, shipping and returns go into the WooCommerce product schema.

    Checked and working 5 checks
    • Product with name, SKU, images and description
    • Offer with EUR price, list price, availability, priceValidUntil and seller
    • aggregateRating and review on reviewed products
    • JSON-LD is in the server HTML, so every crawler sees it
    • Valid JSON, no parse errors on the 8 templates

    High

    Full-size photos make pages 6–12 MB

    • Images are most of the weight: product photos are uploaded as full-size PNG files, such as a 2.2 MB oranzheria-sunny-6x3-dve-vrati.jpg.png.
    • The largest image on the page (the LCP element) is lazy-loaded, so the browser starts it last.

    Fix: serve WebP or AVIF at display size with srcset (an image plugin or CDN does this for existing uploads), and load the first image eagerly with fetchpriority="high".

    Medium

    Tags block the phone, and the home slider jumps

    • Google Tag Manager alone blocks the main thread for 1.5 s on the product page. GA4, Ads, Meta, Yandex, Trustindex and TBI all load at start.
    • The home slider shifts the layout twice (0.35 and 0.27), which gives the 0.636 CLS.

    Fix: reserve the slider's height, and load non-essential tags after consent or the first interaction.

    • Real phones on good connections will load faster than 27–42 s, but the ranking of the problems is the same.
    • The server isn't the problem: the HTML arrives in 20–40 ms.

    Mobile, HTTPS & URLs

    These are baselines. They carry little ranking weight once they're in place.

    6/ 8
    High

    HTTPS isn't enforced, and there are no security headers

    Only the home page redirects from http. There's no HSTS, and none of the usual security headers (Content-Security-Policy, X-Content-Type-Options, X-Frame-Options, Referrer-Policy, Permissions-Policy). For a shop that takes payments and personal data, this is a trust issue as well as an SEO one. Mobile parity and URLs get full marks; HTTPS gets 0 of 2.

    Fix: a site-wide 301 to https, HSTS (start with a short max-age), and the basic headers at the host or in the security plugin.

    Checked and working 6 checks
    • Responsive layout with the viewport meta tag set
    • Same content and markup on mobile and desktop
    • Valid TLS certificate
    • Readable Bulgarian slugs (/продукти/перголи/)
    • Lower-case, stable URLs; upper-case variants return 404
    • No session IDs or random IDs in URLs

    Sitemaps & IndexNow

    Faster discovery and recrawls. Bing ties freshness to Copilot and supports IndexNow. Google doesn't.

    3/ 6
    Medium

    The sitemap index links a missing file

    WordPress's built-in sitemap lists 732 URLs in 9 files. One of them, wp-sitemap-taxonomies-pa_марка-1.xml (brands), returns 404. The index also lists the default "uncategorized" category and taxonomy pages that have no canonical.

    Fix: let the SEO plugin's sitemap replace the core one, with only canonical, indexable URLs in it.

    Low

    No IndexNow

    Bing and Copilot learn about price and stock changes only when they next recrawl. Taxonomy files also have no lastmod, although product lastmod dates are real (83 different days).

    Fix: turn on IndexNow (built into Rank Math, or the IndexNow plugin) so product updates are sent to Bing as they happen.

    Checked and working 4 checks
    • Sitemap declared in robots.txt
    • Split by type: 619 products, 32 categories, 34 pages, 25 posts
    • All 14 sampled sitemap URLs return 200
    • Product lastmod values are real change dates

    AI visibility readiness

    How ready the shop is to be read and cited by AI answers
    74/ 100

    This score measures how easily ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Copilot, Gemini and Google AI Overviews can find, read and cite 24home. Every AI crawler we tested, except Meta's, gets the full server-rendered shop, so the hardest part is already in place. Points are lost on duplicate URLs, missing shop and freshness data, and AI-specific files.

    Fair · 11 of 15

    Meta's crawler is blocked, and training isn't a separate choice

    robots.txt has one User-agent: * group, so every AI crawler is allowed. The firewall, though, answers meta-externalagent (which feeds Meta AI) with HTTP 412 and a 1 KB error. All 25 other agents got a 200. There are no separate rules for training crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended, CCBot), so the shop hasn't decided whether it wants to be in training sets as well as in AI answers.

    Fix: allow meta-externalagent in the firewall if Meta AI visibility is wanted, and write explicit robots.txt groups for training crawlers either way.

    Fair · 8 of 15

    Google and Bing can index the shop, but duplicates dilute it

    Gemini and Google AI Overviews answer from Google's index, and Copilot from Bing's. Both bots get full HTML. Points are lost on the same issues as in the SEO part: categories without canonicals, every page on http as well as https, open sort, brand and price URLs, and no meta descriptions. See Canonicals & duplicate URLs and Facets, filters & variants.

    Fix: the SEO plugin and HTTPS fixes in the plan lift this score too.

    Strong · 10 of 12

    Product pages are rich in text

    Products carry a written description, a specification table ("Допълнителна информация"), reviews and instructions, all in the server HTML. Blog posts use question headings with concrete sizes and materials. Missing: delivery, returns and TBI instalment terms live on separate pages, not on the product, and there's no FAQ text an assistant could quote.

    Fix: a short delivery, returns and instalments block on every product, and a few FAQs per main category.

    Fair · 6 of 10

    A shopping feed probably exists, but we couldn't see it

    The cached Pergolas page holds a Performance Max ad click, which usually means a Merchant Center feed. We found no feed plugin or public feed URL to check it. Price and stock are in the JSON-LD; shipping and returns are not. Google's 2026 AI guide tells shops to use Merchant Center feeds to appear in AI shopping answers.

    Fix: confirm the feed covers all 619 products, add shipping and returns to it and to the markup, and keep an eye on Google's Universal Commerce Protocol.

    Fair · 4 of 7

    Freshness is half there

    Product lastmod dates in the sitemap are real, and blog posts show dates. But there's no dateModified in the markup, taxonomy sitemaps have no lastmod, and there's no IndexNow. AI bots favour recent content: 65% of their hits went to pages from the past year (Seer; correlation, not cause).

    Fix: dateModified on products and posts (the SEO plugin's schema does this), and IndexNow.

    Fair · 3 of 5

    The shop's identity is visible, but not machine-readable

    The footer has everything an assistant needs to recognise the business: phone +359 898 615 252, Facebook (24home.Bulgaria), Instagram (24home.bg), YouTube (@24home33), a map location, and about, delivery, returns and payment pages. None of it is in Organization markup. Bing has said schema helps its LLMs; Google says no special markup is needed, so the weight is modest.

    Fix: Organization (or OnlineStore) on the home page with logo, contact point, address, sameAs links to the three profiles, and shop-wide shipping and returns.

    Fair · 3 of 4

    Most controls are named, a few links are not

    Browsing agents act through the accessibility tree. Buttons have names and focus is visible. But product-card overlay links (a.product__link) and brand-slider links have no text, and the enquiry form's phone field is labelled only by its placeholder (see Accessibility).

    Weak · 0 of 3

    No llms.txt: a cheap missed opportunity

    /llms.txt returns 404. Google says Search doesn't use these files, and SE Ranking found no measurable effect on citations, so the weight is low. They still give AI agents a clean summary of the shop, its categories and its policies.

    Fix: a short /llms.txt with the shop's description, main categories, delivery and returns, and contact details. Some SEO plugins generate it.

    • HTML for AI crawlers, 23 of 23: OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, GPTBot, Claude-SearchBot, Claude-User, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and Perplexity-User all get the full page
    • robots.txt allows all AI search and user agents
    • Googlebot and bingbot get full HTML, so Gemini, AI Overviews and Copilot can read the shop
    • Snippet controls, 4 of 4: no nosnippet or max-snippet limits, and max-image-preview:large

    Consent & Consent Mode v2

    In the EEA, Google Ads requires Consent Mode v2 for remarketing and conversion modelling. GDPR requires that rejecting is as easy as accepting, and that no tracking runs before the visitor chooses.

    5/ 100
    Critical

    No consent banner, and tracking starts on the first page view

    A first-time visitor sees no banner at all. Before any click, the home page sets these cookies:

    • Google: _ga, _ga_8SEHV4T3T1, _gcl_au, and test_cookie and IDE on doubleclick.net.
    • Meta: _fbp.
    • Yandex: _ym_uid, _ym_d, _ym_isad, plus cookies on yandex.com and yandex.ru.

    GA4, Google Ads, Meta and Yandex requests all fire at the same time. The cookie policy page (/политика-за-бисквитки-ес/) is linked in the footer but offers no choice. Leftover styles for a Complianz banner in the theme suggest a consent plugin was removed at some point.

    Fix: a Google-certified consent tool (for example Cookiebot, CookieYes or Complianz) with Accept and Reject on the first layer, analytics and marketing categories, and a "Cookie settings" link in the footer. Nothing loads until the visitor chooses.

    High

    Consent Mode v2 says "granted" by default

    The page sets Consent Mode's default with ad_storage, analytics_storage, ad_user_data and ad_personalization all granted. So Google is told the visitor agreed to ads and analytics when they were never asked.

    Fix: default denied for all four signals, and an update from the consent tool when the visitor chooses.

    Rendering splits the two channels

    Google renders JavaScript, and almost every other AI crawler doesn't. A shop that builds its pages in the browser can pass for Google and still be invisible to ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity. 24home serves full HTML to everyone, which is what both channels need.

    Schema matters for Google, less for AI

    Product and merchant markup still earns rich results in Google. As a lever for AI citations it's disputed: Ahrefs' 2026 controlled test found no lift. What drives AI citations most is off-page (referring domains, brand mentions, YouTube, Reddit), which no site setting can control.

    Methodology & Sources

    How we tested, and what we didn't cover

    What we ran

    • HTTP fetches of one product as a browser, as Googlebot and as 24 other agents, with and without a cache-busting parameter
    • URL-variant, filter, parameter and status-code probes, and a check of the sitemap index, its 9 files and a 14-URL sample
    • Parsing of the server HTML of 8 page templates for meta, canonical, headings, links, images and JSON-LD
    • A real Chrome session from the home page to the cart (one product added, then removed), recording the dataLayer, network calls, cookies and consent state
    • Local Lighthouse 12.8 (4 mobile runs), axe-core 4.10 on 6 pages, and a 24 px target-size measurement at phone width

    What we didn't cover

    • Search Console, Bing AI Performance and index coverage
    • Real-user CrUX data, including INP (the PageSpeed Insights quota was exhausted)
    • Live prompt testing in ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini
    • Checkout, payment and purchase events: no order was placed, and no form was sent
    • The Merchant Center feed, ad accounts and the logged-in account area
