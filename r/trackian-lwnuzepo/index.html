    Crawlability & rendering

    Nothing else matters if the crawler can't see the content. Google recommends server-side rendering, and most AI crawlers don't run JavaScript at all.

    20/ 20

    Every visitor and every crawler gets the same pre-built HTML page: about 67 KB for a feature page, with the full text, links, title, canonical and JSON-LD. We fetched the anomaly-detection feature page as 26 user agents, with and without a cache-busting parameter.

    Canonicals, hreflang & duplicate URLs

    Since Dec 2025, Google has said canonicals and noindex must be right in the first HTML response. On a multilingual site, hreflang tells Google which language version to show, and only works when each version is really translated.

    7/ 9
    Medium

    Integration pages in seven languages are mostly English

    • The 40 integration pages exist in all 8 languages, so about 280 non-English URLs (/bg/integrations/shopify/, /de/integrations/google_analytics_4/ …).
    • Their navigation, H1 label and section headings are translated, but the body text and the meta description stay English: "Shopify is a hosted e-commerce platform that runs online stores…" on the Bulgarian page.
    • Each one is hreflang-linked to the English page, so Google sees eight near-identical versions and may pick one canonical for all of them, or show English text to a German searcher.

    Fix: translate the body and description of the integration pages, starting with the languages that get traffic. Until a page is translated, leave it out of the sitemap and the hreflang set (or point its canonical to the English page), rather than publishing it as a separate language version.

    Checked and working 6 checks
    • Every sampled page carries an absolute self-canonical, in all 8 languages
    • hreflang is reciprocal across en, bg, de, el, es, it, pl, ro, with an x-default
    • English-only resources declare only en and x-default, so no hreflang points to a missing page
    • http:// and www. 301-redirect to https://trackian.com in one hop
    • A missing trailing slash 301-redirects to the slash form
    • Unknown and utm_ parameters keep the canonical to the clean URL

    Internal linking & architecture

    Internal links decide how fast Google finds each page and how much weight it gets. Google's 2026 AI guidance names them explicitly.

    9/ 12
    Medium

    Breadcrumbs exist only in the markup

    Every inner page sends BreadcrumbList JSON-LD (Home › Client Case Studies › Plamax…), but no sampled page shows a breadcrumb trail. Visitors and crawlers lose the link up to the parent section, and integration, case-study and resource pages link back to their hub only through the main menu.

    Fix: show the same breadcrumb visibly under the header on every inner template, using the data already in the JSON-LD.

    Low

    Resource pages have no site navigation

    Lead-magnet pages such as /resources/why-numbers-never-agree/ drop the site header and footer: the page has 5 links in total, 4 of them internal. That suits a landing page, but it makes 90 English resources dead ends for crawlers and keeps their authority from flowing to features and pricing.

    Fix: keep the focused layout, but add a slim footer with links to the resources hub, the related feature and pricing.

    Checked and working 5 checks
    • Crawlable header and footer: 43–46 internal links on every main template
    • An HTML sitemap page (/website-sitemap/) linked from the footer
    • Features, use cases and roles are linked from the home page and the menu
    • The resources hub links to every resource (101 internal links)
    • Integration hub with category filters, all as crawlable links

    Titles, meta & headings

    Titles are a relevance signal, and they drive the click-through rate that Google's NavBoost records. Meta descriptions only affect clicks.

    6/ 10
    Medium

    Key titles name the page, not what it offers

    • Commercial pages: "Pricing | Trackian" (18 characters), "FAQ | Trackian" (14), "About Us | Trackian" (19). Searches like "marketing anomaly detection pricing" or "GA4 alternative" have nothing to match.
    • Resources: the hub is titled just "Resources" (9 characters), and resource pages drop the brand ("Why Your Numbers Never Agree").
    • Translations: integration pages in 7 languages keep the English meta description.

    Fix: titles of 45–60 characters that say what the page gives ("Trackian Pricing: Plans from €59, 14-Day Free Trial"), " | Trackian" on every resource, and translated descriptions.

    Low

    One H1 repeats itself

    On resource pages the H1 holds both the desktop and the mobile copy of the subtitle, so the server HTML reads "Why Your Numbers Never Agree Meta, Google Ads, GA4 and your shop Meta, Google Ads, GA4 and your shop". Crawlers and screen readers get the repeated text.

    Fix: keep only the title in the H1, and put the subtitle in one element outside it.

    Checked and working 5 checks
    • Unique titles on all 17 sampled pages, all written server-side
    • Exactly one H1 per page on every template
    • A meta description on every sampled page
    • Open Graph and Twitter card tags on every page, with article images on posts
    • Feature, use-case and role titles are descriptive ("Data Anomaly Detection | Trackian")

    High

    The first screen takes 10–17 s on a throttled phone

    • The server answers in about 0.2 s and the page itself is light (1.4 MB on home). The delay is JavaScript: 5.1 s of main-thread work on home, 2.9 s of it script evaluation.
    • The hero image arrives in 0.4 s but waits 2.6 s to be painted (element render delay) while scripts run.
    • Lighthouse estimates 387 KB of unused JavaScript, 820 ms of render-blocking CSS, and 401 KB that could be cached longer (the HTML is served with Cache-Control: no-cache, and 24 assets have short cache lifetimes).

    Fix: split the Nuxt bundle so each page loads only its own components, inline the critical CSS, and give hashed /_nuxt/ files a one-year immutable cache.

    Medium

    Marketing tags hold the phone for almost a second

    Third-party code blocks the main thread for 910 ms on home: Google Tag Manager and its tags 856 ms (524 KB), Meta 373 ms (270 KB), Clarity 186 ms. They all start on the first paint, before the visitor has done anything.

    Fix: load marketing tags after consent (which the law needs anyway, see Consent) and after the first interaction or idle time.

    Note

    Reading these numbers

    • Lab data from Lighthouse 12.8 on a throttled mobile profile, one run per page. We had no real-user (CrUX) data or INP.
    • Real phones on good connections will be faster than 10–17 s, but the cause (script work, not server time or weight) is the same.
    • Total Blocking Time stands in for INP in the lab.
    Checked and working 5 checks
    • HTML in about 0.2 s (Apache, static pages)
    • No layout shift: CLS 0 on three of four pages, 0.023 on the feature page
    • Light pages: 1.4 MB on home
    • Images in WebP, with width and height on most
    • Fonts load without hiding text

    Mobile, HTTPS & URLs

    These are baselines. They carry little ranking weight once they're in place.

    7/ 8
    Low

    No HSTS or security headers

    HTTPS is enforced with a 301, but the server sends no Strict-Transport-Security, and none of Content-Security-Policy, X-Content-Type-Options, X-Frame-Options, Referrer-Policy or Permissions-Policy. For a product that connects to customers’ ad and analytics accounts, buyers and their security reviews look for these. Mobile parity and URLs get full marks; HTTPS gets 1 of 2.

    Fix: HSTS (start with a short max-age), and the basic headers in the Apache config.

    Checked and working 6 checks
    • Responsive layout with the viewport meta tag set
    • Same HTML for mobile and desktop agents
    • No horizontal scrolling at 390 px on 5 templates
    • Valid TLS, and http 301-redirects to https
    • Readable, lower-case slugs (/features/anomaly-detection/)
    • Language folders for every translation (/de/, /bg/ …)

    Sitemaps & IndexNow

    Faster discovery and recrawls. Bing ties freshness to Copilot and supports IndexNow. Google doesn't.

    4/ 6
    Medium

    No lastmod dates, and no IndexNow found

    The sitemap lists 697 URLs with 4,897 hreflang alternates, but no lastmod on any of them. Google and Bing can’t tell an updated pricing page from one that hasn’t changed in a year. We found no IndexNow key, so Bing and Copilot learn about changes only on their next crawl.

    Fix: write the real last-change date of each page into lastmod at build time, and send changed URLs to IndexNow from the deploy step.

    Checked and working 4 checks
    • Sitemap declared in robots.txt
    • Every URL in it is canonical and returns 200 (sample of 14 across languages)
    • hreflang alternates for all 8 languages inside the sitemap
    • No staging, parameter or 404 URLs in it

    How ready the site is to be read and cited by AI answers
    87/ 100

    This score measures how easily ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Copilot, Gemini and Google AI Overviews can find, read and cite Trackian. The site is ahead of most we test: every AI crawler gets the full page, /llms.txt explains the product and when to use it, every page answers Accept: text/markdown with a Markdown version, and an MCP server lets assistants work with customers’ own data. Points are lost on freshness signals and on offers that exist only as text.

    Strong · 13 of 15

    Training crawlers aren’t a separate choice

    robots.txt has one group, User-agent: * / Allow: /, so every AI search, user and training crawler is allowed, and all 26 agents got a 200. That’s the right default for visibility. What’s missing is a deliberate decision about training crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended, CCBot), which are separate from the agents that fetch pages to answer questions.

    Fix: add explicit robots.txt groups for the training crawlers, allowing or disallowing them on purpose. Keep OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, Claude-SearchBot, Claude-User and PerplexityBot allowed.

    Fix: the translation and title fixes in the plan lift this score too.

    Strong · 11 of 12

    Facts are in text; case studies are thin

    Features, use cases and roles explain the product in headings and short paragraphs, with FAQs written as text. Pricing lists every plan, price and limit as text. The Markdown versions strip the layout for agents. The gap is the case studies: the Plamax page has 192 words and no subheadings, so the "over 300% ROI" claim has no method or numbers an assistant could quote.

    Fix: give each case study a short structure (starting point, what changed, numbers with dates) of 500 words or more.

    Strong · 8 of 10

    Plans are readable, but not structured

    The SaaS version of this row asks whether pricing and documentation are in readable text. They are: three plans with prices, seats, projects and data retention on /pricing/, and MCP setup steps on /features/ai-mcp-api/. What’s missing is the same data as Offer markup, which shopping-style and comparison answers can read directly.

    Fair · 3 of 7

    No dates anywhere a machine can read

    The sitemap has no lastmod, articles, resources and case studies show no visible dates, and the insights’ BlogPosting has datePublished but no dateModified. No IndexNow key was found. AI bots favour recent content: 65% of their hits went to pages from the past year (Seer; correlation, not cause).

    Fix: real lastmod in the sitemap, a visible "Updated" date and dateModified on articles, resources and case studies, and IndexNow on deploy.

    Strong · 4 of 5

    The company is described, but not linked to its profiles

    The home page’s Organization has the name, logo, a description, the Svilengrad address, email and phone, and the about and contact pages exist. It has no sameAs, so assistants can’t tie the site to Trackian’s LinkedIn, Crunchbase, G2 or GitHub profiles and the 2025 European Search Awards shortlisting.

    Fix: add sameAs links to every official profile, and foundingDate and founder if they are public.

    Medium

    The site leaves lead tracking entirely to GTM triggers

    • The page itself pushes no named events: GTM has to guess a lead from generic form-interaction and click triggers.
    • GA4’s recommended lead events (form_start, generate_lead, sign_up) carry no lead ID or form name that we could see, so Ads and Meta can’t be told which demo turned into a customer.

    Fix: push form_start and, on a confirmed submission, generate_lead with a lead ID and form name into the dataLayer; send sign_up from the app, and confirm cross-domain measurement between the two domains.

    Fix: submit one test request in GTM preview mode and confirm the event, its parameters and that it fires once.

    Consent & Consent Mode v2

    In the EEA, Google Ads requires Consent Mode v2 for remarketing and conversion modelling. GDPR requires that rejecting is as easy as accepting, and that no tracking runs before the visitor chooses.

    4/ 100
    Critical

    No consent banner, and tracking starts on the first page view

    A first-time visitor sees no banner at all. Before any click, the home page sets these cookies:

    • Google: _ga, _ga_X1M5G0YPZR, _gcl_au, plus _gcl_ls in local storage.
    • Meta: _fbp. Reddit: _rdt_uuid. Clarity: its session cookies.

    Requests to GA4, Google Ads (three endpoints), Meta, Reddit, Clarity and the AWS events endpoint all fire at once. The footer links the Privacy Policy and Cookie Policy, but offers no choice. For a Bulgarian company that sells marketing measurement to EU businesses, this is also a credibility risk: prospects who check will notice.

    Fix: a Google-certified consent tool (for example Cookiebot, CookieYes or Usercentrics) with Accept and Reject on the first layer, analytics and marketing categories, and a "Cookie settings" link in the footer. Nothing loads until the visitor chooses.

    High

    Consent Mode v2 says "granted" by default

    The first dataLayer entry sets Consent Mode’s default with ad_storage, analytics_storage, ad_user_data and ad_personalization all granted, with no region. So Google is told every visitor, in the EU too, agreed to ads and analytics when they were never asked.

    Fix: default denied for all four signals (at least for the EEA, UK and Switzerland), and an update from the consent tool when the visitor chooses.

    Checked and working 3 checks
    • Privacy Policy and Cookie Policy pages exist and are linked in the footer
    • Consent Mode is already wired in with all four v2 signals; only its default values are wrong
    • A privacy centre (/privacy-center/) groups the policies and terms

    Rendering splits the two channels

    Google renders JavaScript, and almost every other AI crawler doesn't. A site that builds its pages in the browser can pass for Google and still be invisible to ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity. Trackian serves static HTML to everyone, which is what both channels need.

    Schema matters for Google, less for AI

    Software and offer markup still earns rich results in Google. As a lever for AI citations it's disputed: Ahrefs' 2026 controlled test found no lift. What drives AI citations most is off-page (referring domains, brand mentions, YouTube, Reddit), which no site setting can control.

    Methodology & Sources

    How we tested, and what we didn't cover

    What we ran

    • HTTP fetches of one feature page as a browser, as Googlebot and as 24 other agents, with and without a cache-busting parameter, plus a request for its Markdown version
    • URL-variant, parameter and status-code probes, a check of the sitemap (697 URLs, 4,897 hreflang alternates) and a 14-URL sample across languages
    • Parsing of the server HTML of 17 pages (15 English templates, a Bulgarian feature page and German pricing) for meta, canonical, hreflang, headings, links, images and JSON-LD, and spot checks of matching pages in other languages
    • A real Chrome session from the home page to the demo form (fields focused, nothing sent), recording the dataLayer, network calls, cookies and consent state
    • Local Lighthouse 12.8 (4 mobile runs), axe-core 4.10 on 6 pages at desktop width and 5 at phone width
    • Scored as a SaaS and lead-generation site: "Facets & variants" became URL parameters & archives, "Product feeds" became Structured offers, the schema row asks for SoftwareApplication and Offer markup, and the tracking funnel is the lead and sign-up funnel

    What we didn't cover

    • Search Console, Bing AI Performance and index coverage
    • Real-user CrUX data, including INP (Lighthouse lab data only)
    • Live prompt testing in ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini
    • Demo, contact and free-check form submissions, so the lead event itself; sign-up and the app on trackian.app
    • A full translation review of the seven other languages (spot checks only), ad accounts and the GTM container
    • Whether an IndexNow key exists (it can sit at any path, so we can only say none was found)
